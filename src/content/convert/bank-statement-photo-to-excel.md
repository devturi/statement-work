## When a photo is all you have

Photos are how a lot of statements arrive in practice: a client snaps the paper statement on the kitchen table, or a small business owner photographs last year's statements from a folder. There's no text in a photo to copy, so the usual answer is to retype it. statement.work reads the image instead, using the same statement-aware extraction it uses for scans and PDFs.

## How to take a photo that converts well

The quality of the photo decides the quality of the result. Five habits make the biggest difference:

1. **Lay the page flat** on a dark, plain surface. Curled pages bend the rows.
2. **Shoot straight down**, with the phone parallel to the paper. Angled shots squeeze one side of the page.
3. **Use even light.** Daylight from a window works well. Avoid the flash, which causes glare on glossy paper, and keep your shadow off the page.
4. **Fill the frame with one page**, edge to edge, including the header with the statement period and the balances.
5. **Hold steady and check focus** before moving on. Zoom in on the photo and make sure small digits are sharp.

If your phone's camera or notes app has a document-scanning mode, it will usually straighten and crop the page for you, which helps.

## Converting the photos

1. Upload the photos to statement.work, one image per page, in order.
2. statement.work extracts each transaction: date, description, amount, running balance and a category.
3. Review the result and export to Excel or CSV.

## Checking a photo conversion

Photos vary more than PDFs, so a quick check is worth it:

- **Balances:** opening balance plus money in minus money out should equal the closing balance.
- **Row count:** the number of transactions should match the statement.
- **Running balance chain:** each balance should follow from the previous one. A break points straight at the row to look at.

If a page converts poorly, the fastest fix is usually a better photo of that page rather than manual correction.

## Photo, scan or PDF?

If the bank offers PDF statements in online banking, download those: they contain real text and convert most reliably. If you only have paper, a [flatbed scan](/convert/scanned-bank-statement-ocr/) is more consistent than a photo. A photo is the right choice when it's what you've got, and statement.work treats all three the same way and produces the same columns from each.

## Who uses photo conversion

[Small businesses](/for/small-businesses/) catching up on paper records, and bookkeepers whose clients send pictures instead of files. Once converted, the data can go to [Excel](/convert/bank-statement-pdf-to-excel/), CSV or JSON like any other statement.
