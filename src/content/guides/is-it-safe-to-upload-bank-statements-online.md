Uploading a bank statement online can be safe if the service encrypts it in transit and at rest, keeps it no longer than needed, uses it only to do the job you asked for, and you handle the downloaded results carefully. The risk isn't the upload itself; it's not knowing what happens next. Use the checklist below for any tool, including ours.

## What's sensitive on a statement

A statement is more revealing than it looks:

- **Identity:** name, address, sometimes date of birth or customer numbers.
- **Account details:** account number and sort code or routing details, often partly masked, but not always.
- **Financial picture:** balances, income, spending habits, employers, landlords, health and family payments.
- **Third parties:** names of the people and businesses who pay and are paid.

For accountants and bookkeepers, it's also *someone else's* data, which brings professional and often legal duties.

## Questions to ask before uploading

### Transport and storage

- [ ] **Is the connection encrypted?** The address should be `https://` with no browser warnings.
- [ ] **Is data encrypted at rest?** Stored files should be encrypted, not just protected by a login.

### Retention

- [ ] **How long are uploaded files kept?** "Until you delete them" and "forever" are very different from "deleted after processing".
- [ ] **Can you control retention?** Being able to set or shorten it is a strong signal.
- [ ] **What about the outputs?** Converted data may be stored separately from the original file.

### Use of your data

- [ ] **Is your data used to train AI models or improve the service?** Many services' terms allow it unless you opt out. Look for a clear statement either way.
- [ ] **Is it shared with third parties?** Look for a list of sub-processors (the vendors who handle data on the service's behalf).
- [ ] **Is it sold or used for advertising?** For financial documents, the answer should be an unambiguous no.

### Who's behind it

- [ ] **Is there an identifiable company** with a privacy policy and terms you can read?
- [ ] **Is there a way to ask security questions** and get answers before you upload?
- [ ] **Does your firm need more?** Formal reviews may ask about hosting location, access controls and certifications. Ask for documentation.

### Account security

- [ ] **Is multi-factor authentication available** on accounts that hold statements?
- [ ] **Can you control who on your team has access?**

## Red flags

- No privacy policy, or one that doesn't mention uploaded documents.
- Vague retention ("we may retain data as needed").
- Rights to use uploads "to improve our services" with no way to opt out.
- A free service with no clear business model, handling financial documents.
- Pressure to upload before you can read the terms.

## Safe habits on your side

Most statement data leaks don't involve the tool at all. They involve email, shared folders and downloads.

1. **Get statements securely.** Download from online banking yourself, or ask clients to use a secure file-sharing method rather than plain email attachments.
2. **Upload only what's needed.** One month for a one-month question.
3. **Treat exports like the statement.** A CSV of transactions is just as sensitive as the PDF. Store it in access-controlled locations.
4. **Name files carefully** (client, account, period) to avoid sending the wrong file to the wrong person.
5. **Clean up downloads.** Delete local copies you no longer need, including the downloads folder.
6. **Use strong, unique passwords and MFA** everywhere statements live.
7. **Mask what you don't need.** If a task doesn't need the account number, don't include it in what you share onward.

## Is it safer than the alternative?

The realistic alternative to an upload is often a statement emailed between people, retyped into a spreadsheet saved on a laptop, and copied to a shared drive. That has risks too: more copies, more places, more people. A tool with clear encryption, retention and no-training commitments can reduce the number of copies rather than add to them, if you also handle the outputs carefully.

## Summary

Check encryption, retention, use of data and who's behind the service before uploading. Then keep the outputs as safe as the originals. A service that answers those questions plainly is one you can evaluate; one that doesn't isn't.
