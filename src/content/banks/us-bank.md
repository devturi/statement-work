## About U.S. Bank statements

U.S. Bank is the main banking subsidiary of U.S. Bancorp, headquartered in Minneapolis, with a large customer base across the Midwest and West. Many small businesses bank there, so its business checking statements are a regular part of bookkeeping work.

## What the statements typically contain

- Account and statement period details at the top.
- A **balance summary** with beginning balance, deposits, withdrawals and ending balance.
- Transactions listed with dates (month/day), descriptions and amounts, often grouped as deposits, withdrawals and checks.
- Fee and interest information, and for business accounts, analysis or service charge details where they apply.

Your own statements are the reference; layouts change by account type and over time.

## Common conversion problems

- **ACH descriptions.** Electronic payments carry long descriptions such as company name, entry description and an ID number. Accurate as a record, but unhelpful for grouping. statement.work cleans descriptions and assigns a category, while you keep the original detail for reference.
- **Grouped deposits and withdrawals.** Like many US statements, activity may be split by type. A single chronological table is easier to reconcile.
- **Checks listed separately.** Check images or check lists can follow the main transactions and need to be merged back in.
- **Business service charges.** Fees on business accounts can be summarized separately from the transaction list. Watch for them in the totals.

## How to convert it

1. Download the PDF statement from online banking, or use a scan or photo.
2. Upload it to statement.work.
3. Review the transactions and categories.
4. Export to [CSV](/convert/bank-statement-pdf-to-csv/) or Excel.
5. Confirm the ending balance agrees with the statement.

## Make categories consistent

Payroll, vendor payments and tax payments via ACH are where good categories pay off. If you want your categories to match across banks and clients, use a fixed list.
