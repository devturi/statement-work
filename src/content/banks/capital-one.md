## About Capital One statements

Capital One, headquartered in McLean, Virginia, is one of the largest credit card issuers in the US and also offers checking and savings accounts. In practice, the Capital One statements that reach bookkeepers and accountants are very often credit card statements, which work differently from bank account statements.

## What card statements typically contain

- A **summary**: previous balance, payments and credits, purchases, fees, interest and new balance.
- Payment information: minimum payment and due date.
- Transactions with dates, merchant descriptions and amounts, sometimes grouped by cardholder on accounts with several cards.
- Interest and fee details.

Bank account statements follow the more familiar pattern of opening balance, deposits, withdrawals and closing balance. Your own statements are the reference for exact layouts.

## Common conversion problems

- **No running balance.** Card statements rarely show a balance after each transaction, so the check has to use the summary totals rather than a row-by-row chain.
- **Signs.** On a card, purchases increase what you owe and payments reduce it. Keep a consistent sign convention before combining card and bank data.
- **Payments double-counted.** The card payment shows on the card statement and as a withdrawal on the bank statement. Count it once, as a transfer.
- **Several cardholders.** Grouping by cardholder can split the transactions into blocks that need to be put back in date order.
- **Fees and interest lines.** Small, easy to miss, and worth flagging. See [hidden fees](/guides/how-to-spot-duplicate-charges-and-hidden-fees/).

## How to convert it

1. Download the statement PDF, or use a scan or photo.
2. Upload it to statement.work.
3. Review purchases, payments, fees and interest as dated rows.
4. Export to Excel or CSV.
5. Check the new balance using the summary formula above.
