## About Monzo statements

Monzo is a UK app-based bank, founded in 2015, with personal and business accounts managed almost entirely through its mobile app. App-based banks tend to give account holders good digital exports, so the main reason to convert Monzo statements is a different one: the statement has reached you as a PDF.

## When you'll still have PDFs

- **Accountants and bookkeepers** often receive statements from clients as PDFs rather than having access to the account.
- **Lenders, landlords and agencies** ask for PDF statements, and those are the files people keep.
- **Mixed-bank work** is simpler when every bank, app-based or not, ends up in the same columns.

## What the statements typically contain

- Account details and the statement period.
- A list of transactions with date, description, amount and balance.
- Transfers to and from Pots, where used, alongside card payments, bank transfers and direct debits.

Check your own statements for the exact layout, which can change as the app evolves.

## Common conversion problems

- **Pot transfers.** Moving money into a savings Pot looks like money leaving the account. Categorize it as a transfer, not spending.
- **Many small transactions.** App-banking customers often have long statements full of small card payments. Manual entry is especially slow here.
- **Merchant names.** Descriptions are usually cleaner than on traditional bank statements, which helps categorization.

## How to convert it

1. Use the PDF statement you have, or download one from the app.
2. Upload it to statement.work.
3. Review the transactions, marking Pot movements as transfers.
4. Export to [CSV](/convert/bank-statement-pdf-to-csv/) or Excel. The [CSV vs XLSX guide](/guides/csv-vs-xlsx-for-bank-data/) helps you choose.
5. Check the closing balance.
