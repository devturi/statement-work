## About Lloyds Bank statements

Lloyds Bank is one of the UK's largest high-street banks and part of Lloyds Banking Group, which also includes Halifax and Bank of Scotland. Lloyds serves a large number of personal and business customers, so its statements are a regular part of UK bookkeeping.

## What the statements typically contain

- Account details (sort code and account number) and the statement period.
- An opening balance, and totals for money in and money out.
- A transaction table with date, description, **type**, money in, money out and balance.
- Short payment-type codes on many transactions, with a key on the statement.

Formats differ between personal and business accounts and change over time, so treat this as a guide.

## Common conversion problems

- **Payment type column.** A separate type column (debit card, Faster Payment, direct debit and so on) is useful but shifts other values when copied as text. statement.work keeps the transaction and its amount together.
- **Money in and money out columns.** Two amount columns become one signed amount, so totals and pivots work.
- **Statements from several group brands.** A client may have a Lloyds business account and a [Halifax](/banks/halifax/) personal account. They look different but should end up in the same columns.
- **Multi-page balances.** Balances repeated at page breaks are not transactions.

## How to convert it

1. Download the statement PDF from online banking, or use a scan or photo.
2. Upload it to statement.work.
3. Review each transaction's date, description, amount, balance and category.
4. Export to [Excel](/convert/bank-statement-pdf-to-excel/) or CSV.
5. Check the closing balance.

## Using payment types for categories

Payment types are a strong hint for [categorization](/guides/how-to-categorize-bank-transactions/). Direct debits are mostly recurring bills, card payments are day-to-day spending, and Faster Payments in are often customer receipts. For another UK bank with type codes, see [HSBC UK](/banks/hsbc-uk/).
