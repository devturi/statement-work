## About Halifax statements

Halifax is a UK bank brand that is part of Lloyds Banking Group. It is best known for personal current accounts, savings and mortgages, which means Halifax statements often reach accountants from sole traders and landlords whose business income and spending runs through a personal account.

## What the statements typically contain

- Your account details (sort code and account number) and the statement period.
- Opening balance, money in and money out totals, and closing balance.
- A transaction table with date, description, payment type, money in, money out and balance.
- Short payment-type codes for many transactions, with a key on the statement.

Check your own statements for details; designs vary and change over time.

## Common conversion problems

- **Mixed business and personal spending.** The conversion is the easy part. The real work is deciding which transactions are business. A category on every row makes that a sorting job rather than a reading job.
- **Payment-type columns.** An extra column between description and amounts shifts values when copied. statement.work keeps each amount with its transaction.
- **Rent and mortgage flows.** For landlords, rent received and mortgage payments on the same account need clear categories to total correctly.
- **Paper statements.** Older records are often paper. A clear [photo](/convert/bank-statement-photo-to-excel/) or scan of each page works.

## How to convert it

1. Download statement PDFs from online banking, or scan or photograph paper statements.
2. Upload them to statement.work.
3. Review the transactions and mark business versus personal with categories.
4. Export to Excel or CSV.
5. Check the closing balances.

## For tax returns

If you're preparing a return from a personal account, convert the full tax year, categorize, and total the business categories. The [tax preparation page](/for/tax-preparation/) and the [categorization guide](/guides/how-to-categorize-bank-transactions/) walk through it. If you also bank with [Lloyds](/banks/lloyds/), its statements convert to the same columns.
