## About NatWest statements

NatWest is one of the UK's largest banks and part of NatWest Group, which also includes Royal Bank of Scotland and Ulster Bank. It has a large base of business customers, from sole traders to established firms, so NatWest business statements are common in UK bookkeeping.

## What the statements typically contain

- Account details (sort code and account number) and the statement period.
- A brought-forward balance at the start.
- A table of transactions with date, description, paid out, paid in and balance.
- Day-first dates, often printed once for several transactions on the same day.
- For business accounts, charges and any account fees for the period.

Your own statements are the definitive reference; designs vary and change.

## Common conversion problems

- **Date carried down.** When the date is printed only once per day, copied rows lose their date. statement.work fills every row.
- **Split amount columns.** Paid out and paid in become a single signed amount.
- **Business charges.** Account and transaction charges may be summarized for the period and applied as one debit. They are easy to miss and worth checking in [our fees guide](/guides/how-to-spot-duplicate-charges-and-hidden-fees/).
- **Takings and settlements.** Card takings typically arrive as batched settlements, so match them to your card provider's reports, not individual sales.

## How to convert it

1. Download the PDF statement from online banking, or use a scan or photo.
2. Upload it to statement.work.
3. Review the transactions and categories.
4. Export as CSV for import or Excel for review.
5. Check that the closing balance matches.
