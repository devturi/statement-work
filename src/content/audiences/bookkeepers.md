## Where bookkeeping time really goes

Bookkeeping software is good at handling transactions once it has them. The slow part is getting them in when a bank feed isn't available, has gaps, or a client has only paper or PDF statements. Then the job becomes copying, retyping and fixing columns, statement by statement, before any categorizing or reconciling can begin.

statement.work removes that step. You give it the statements; it gives you transactions.

## A month-end routine with statement.work

1. **Gather the month's statements** for each account, in whatever form the client sends them.
2. **Convert them together.** Every transaction comes out with date, description, amount, running balance and a category.
3. **Check the balances.** Opening balance plus money in minus money out equals the closing balance.
4. **Export CSV** and import it into your bookkeeping software, using the same column mapping every month.
5. **Reconcile** against the books. Our [reconciliation guide](/guides/how-bookkeepers-reconcile-bank-statements-faster/) has a fast routine.
6. **Review flagged items**: duplicate charges, fee spikes and unusual transactions.

## Catch-up and clean-up jobs

Catch-up work is where manual entry hurts most: a new client arrives with eight months of unfiled statements from two banks. Converting them in one batch gives you a single, consistent dataset to work from. Because every statement produces the same columns, the months stack straight into one spreadsheet, ready to sort, categorize and import.

## Categories that save a pass

Automatic categorization (payroll, rent, subscriptions and so on) means you start from a first draft rather than a blank column. You still decide how categories map to the client's [chart of accounts](/glossary/#chart-of-accounts), but the bulk of obvious transactions are already grouped. For building a category list that stays consistent across clients, see [how to categorize bank transactions](/guides/how-to-categorize-bank-transactions/).

## Questions without spreadsheets

Clients ask questions like "How much did we spend on software this quarter?" or "Did that supplier charge us twice?" With statement.work you can ask those questions in plain words and get an answer built from the actual transactions, without building a pivot table first.

## What it doesn't do

statement.work doesn't post transactions into your accounting software for you, and it doesn't claim built-in integrations with specific products. It produces clean CSV, Excel or JSON that you import using your software's own bank-statement import. The [CSV import guide](/guides/import-bank-statement-csv-into-accounting-software/) covers the general steps.

## Getting started

statement.work is onboarding a first group of bookkeepers. A good test is your messiest client: the one with scans, multiple banks and a backlog.
