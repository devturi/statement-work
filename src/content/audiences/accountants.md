## The problem accountants actually have

Most accountants don't need help with the accounting. They need help with what comes before it. Client records arrive in whatever form the client had to hand: PDF statements from three banks, a scan of last March, a phone photo of a credit card statement. Before any real work can start, someone has to get those transactions into a spreadsheet, check they are complete, and put them in a consistent shape.

That step doesn't need professional judgement, but it eats professional time, and it is where transcription errors creep in.

## How statement.work fits the workflow

1. **Collect** statements from the client in whatever form they come: digital PDFs, scans or photos.
2. **Convert** them in one batch. statement.work extracts every transaction with date, description, amount and running balance, and categorizes it.
3. **Check** completeness with the opening and closing balances and the running balance chain.
4. **Export** to Excel for review or CSV for import into your accounting software.
5. **Ask** questions in plain words when you need a quick answer, like "What did they pay in bank fees this year?"

## Consistency is the real win

When every client's statements come out with the same columns, your downstream work gets simpler. A review spreadsheet template, a set of pivot tables or an import mapping can be reused across clients instead of rebuilt for each bank. That consistency is hard to get from copy-paste, and it is what lets work be shared across a team.

## Anomalies surface on their own

Duplicate charges, fee spikes and unusual transactions are flagged instead of buried in a long statement. For an accountant, that is a head start on the questions you'd ask the client anyway. Our guide to [spotting duplicate charges and hidden fees](/guides/how-to-spot-duplicate-charges-and-hidden-fees/) shows what to look for.

## Handling client data

Client statements are among the most sensitive documents a practice holds. statement.work encrypts them in transit and at rest, deletes source files after processing on a retention window you control, and never uses them to train models. If your firm needs more than that for a vendor review, raise it during early access. See [security](/security/) for detail.
